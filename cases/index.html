How complex questions are investigated

Three anonymised examples focused on the analytical design, the work performed, and the limits of the available evidence.

01 / B2C social platform

Measuring an offline campaign without direct exposure data

Context

An airport out-of-home campaign was intended to prompt existing users to record upcoming travel. Individual ad exposure was not observable and a randomised control group was unavailable.

What I did

  1. Built a transparent exposure proxy from airport-area app activity followed by activity abroad, while excluding local residents and nearby overland destinations.
  2. Defined pre-campaign and campaign windows and compared likely exposed users with a non-airport control population across multiple years.
  3. Separated reach from conversion and segmented users by tenure and prior engagement to test whether changes were behavioural or compositional.
  4. Ran sensitivity checks with alternative attribution windows, an excluded pull-forward period, other airports, and reweighted user mixes.

Techniques

  • Difference-in-Differences
  • Proxy exposure modelling
  • Cohort segmentation
  • Sensitivity analysis
  • Composition checks

Key limitation

Exposure remained inferred rather than observed. The analysis therefore provided directional causal evidence, not experimental proof.

02 / Financial institution

Separating campaign effects from customer journey timing

Context

A temporary pricing campaign offered business customers an additional personal payment product through several email waves. The analysis needed to separate message sequence, pricing, and natural purchase timing.

What I did

  1. Linked masked customer, product, and campaign histories in SQL and reconstructed the paths created by launch, reminder, and follow-up communications.
  2. Built acquisition cohorts with a standardised twelve-month observation period to compare product adoption and purchase timing across years.
  3. Modelled time to purchase in R with right-censored observations, Kaplan-Meier curves, a log-rank test, and a Cox proportional-hazards model.
  4. Built a pre-campaign Poisson baseline to compare expected and observed purchase patterns across both recent and established cohorts.

Techniques

  • SQL data modelling
  • Journey-path analysis
  • Kaplan-Meier analysis
  • Log-rank testing
  • Cox proportional hazards
  • Poisson regression

Key limitation

Reminder recipients were not randomly selected. Differences between communication paths could therefore reflect prior customer interest as well as campaign influence.

03 / Financial institution

Turning delayed registrations into a current market-share signal

Context

Market share among newly registered sole proprietors could only be observed after entrepreneurs opened a business account. That delay made recent months incomplete and unsuitable for direct comparison with mature cohorts.

What I did

  1. Matched external company registrations to internal open and closed account records using unique business identifiers and aligned eligibility rules across numerator and denominator.
  2. Analysed the delay between company registration and account opening and defined a fixed sixty-day maturation window with intermediate measurement points.
  3. Tested whether early conversion could predict the mature market-share estimate and used the model to nowcast recent, incomplete cohorts.
  4. Designed a reporting approach that separates mature observations, forecasts, late conversions, pending applications, and the early indicator used for day-to-day steering.

Techniques

  • External and internal data matching
  • Cohort maturation
  • Lag analysis
  • Linear regression
  • Nowcasting
  • Moving averages

Key limitation

The estimate depends on assumptions about the eligible market and application exclusions. Early conversion is useful for steering, but does not fully determine the mature estimate.
